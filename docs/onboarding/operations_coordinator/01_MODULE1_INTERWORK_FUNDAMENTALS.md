## What InterWork does

InterWork Office Solutions is a commercial furniture moving and installation company. Clients are mostly corporate facilities/real estate teams managing office space.

Services:
- Office relocations — full moves, phased moves, internal restacks
- Furniture decommissions — pickup, disposal, donation coordination
- Furniture installations and service calls — workstations, monitor arms, media carts, whiteboards
- Site walkthroughs and inventory projects
- IT equipment relocation — switches, firewalls, access points (physical handling, not IT/network configuration)
- Storage and asset management
- Work completion reporting
- Vendor and crew coordination
- Disposal, recycling, and sustainability coordination
- Client-facing scheduling and status updates

Two InterWork addresses you'll hear constantly:
- **Warehouse** — 439 Commerce Lane, West Berlin, NJ 08091. Staging, storage, asset holding, pickup/delivery coordination. Not disclosed to clients unless it's actually relevant to them.
- **Corporate/billing office** — 100 Springdale Road, Cherry Hill, NJ 08003-3300.

## Common project types and terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Relocation / Move | Full office move from one address to another |
| Decom | Decommission — remove and dispose of furniture or assets |
| Install | Deliver and install furniture at a location |
| Service Call | Single-task visit (repair, swap, minor adjustment) |
| Walkthrough | Site visit for inventory, scoping, or inspection |
| Punch List | Follow-up visit to complete outstanding items |
| FF | FastField — the field form the on-site PM submits after work |
| WC Report | Work Completion Report — delivered to the client after job closure |
| HA Desk | Height-Adjustable Desk |
| LBC | Locker Base Controller (Vecos SMART locker system — comes up on that specific client's projects) |
| COI | Certificate of Insurance — often required by a building before crew access |
| WHS | Warehouse (see address above) |

A single project can combine more than one of these — a decommission that also includes a furniture delivery to a different address, for example. You'll see this in Module 2.

## Who is who — internal roles

**Francisco Vinueza — Operations Manager.** Direct manager for Alejandro, Scott, and Matt. He is the primary escalation point for field issues, unresolved operational problems, and project guidance — if something is stuck, wrong, or beyond what a project card can resolve, this is who it goes to. (This is a reporting-line fact, separate from the project-level roles below — Francisco can also show up in the Office PM role on a specific project card, but that's a different thing from who manages you.)

The roles that matter structurally on a given project:

**Account Manager** — owns the client relationship and the quote. Issues the quote, gets the project number assigned, and is usually the first point of contact when a client asks for something. (e.g., Jill Buchman handles quoting and client relationships across many accounts.)

**Office PM (Office/Administrative Owner)** — manages the project administratively: scheduling, client communications, keeping the Supabase record current. Doesn't have to be on-site. (Alejandro and Francisco Vinueza most often hold this role.)

**Field PM (Field/Execution Lead)** — the on-site lead who runs the crew and submits the FastField form after the work is done. A project can have both an office PM and a field PM as different people, or the same person can hold both roles — check the project card, don't assume.

**Client Contact / Site POC** — the person(s) at the client company. There is often more than one per project: a facilities contact who requested the work, and a separate on-site contact physically present the day of execution. They are not always the same person.

**Building Contact** — property management at the job site, separate from the client's own staff. Comes up mainly around Certificates of Insurance and after-hours or freight-elevator access — a building can require its own sign-off independent of what the client company has confirmed.

**Crew / Vendor** — the people physically doing the labor. Sometimes InterWork's own field PM and crew, sometimes a third-party vendor company. A "vendor" can also mean something narrower than a full crew — e.g. a company that supplies a rental truck but not labor. Vendor names and rates are internal information — never shared with clients (see Communication Rules, referenced again in Module 2).

One InterWork person can span roles across their career, but on any single project card, each role is usually a specific named person — that's what you're confirming when you read a project.

## What successful project coordination looks like

- The project number is treated as the single identifier for everything about that job — quote, schedule, communications, field form, closeout. If you're not sure of it, that's the first thing to confirm, before anything else.
- Every role (account manager, office PM, field PM, client contact, building contact, crew/vendor) is identified by name before execution day, not discovered on the day.
- Confirmations (client, vendor, building access) are tracked explicitly as confirmed or not — never assumed.
- Unconfirmed information is marked "needs confirmation," not treated as fact and not silently guessed.
- When two pieces of information disagree, that gets flagged and resolved — it doesn't get quietly picked one way.
- Client-facing communication and internal communication are different in tone and content — see Module 2 for the specific rules.
- A clean handoff means the next person (another coordinator, a field PM, even you a week later) can pick up the project from what's written down, without having to reconstruct it from memory.
